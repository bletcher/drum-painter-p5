User Guide

🚀 Quick Start

Drum Painter turns your MIDI drum kit into a paintbrush. Every hit paints on the canvas — the drum picks the color and shape, how hard you hit sets the size.

1️⃣ Plug in your kit and allow MIDI

  • Connect your e-drum module (or any MIDI controller) by USB.
  • Click Allow when the browser asks for MIDI access. The status box at the top of the left panel shows your device when it's connected.
  • MIDI works in Chrome, Edge, Opera and Firefox on a computer. Firefox asks you to approve a small site-permission add-on the first time.

2️⃣ Click “🥁 Kit Setup” and hit each drum when asked

  • The wizard names a drum, you hit that pad, and it moves on to the next one.
  • Pads use General MIDI note numbers by default, so many kits work without any setup — try playing first.

3️⃣ Play!

  • Each hit paints. Try different Freeform styles or switch to Timeline for patterns laid out in time.
  • Space records a video, S saves an image, Backspace clears (undo with Ctrl/⌘+Z).
No kit handy? Click ⌨️ Keyboard pads (or press `) and play with the keys A S D F G H J K L ; — hold Shift for an accent. Or click ▶ Demo beat to hear and see a groove.
💡 Tip: Start with Freeform → Classic and experiment with blend modes. Try Dendritic for river-like patterns, Lightning for electric branches, or Timeline → Fibonacci for geometric growth.

📖 Complete User Guide

MIDI Setup

Kit Setup: Click 🥁 Kit Setup and hit each drum when asked. You can also set a drum's note by hand, or click its 🎛 Learn button, in Customize Drums.

Direct mode (default): each drum is mapped to a MIDI note (and optionally a specific device). Hit a pad → paint appears.
Bank mode: pads select which drum you're editing and faders/knobs control that drum's parameters. Turn it on with the switch at the top of MIDI Mapping.

Paint Modes

Pick Freeform or Timeline with the radio buttons, then choose a style or layout from the dropdown.

Freeform (random placement)

  • Classic: random placement, pointillist style. Supports all shapes.
  • Gravity: falling dots with velocity-dependent drift and fading strokes. Complex shapes become circles.
  • Organic: natural clustering around migrating seed points. Supports all shapes.
  • Dendritic: river-like networks with hierarchical branching. Complex shapes become circles.
  • Lightning: cloud-to-ground discharge with fractal branching. Complex shapes become circles.

Timeline — time-based layouts (sync with the metronome)

  • Staff: horizontal, DAW-style lanes on a musical grid (eighth-note triplets by default).
  • Spiral: notes land where/when they're played along an expanding spiral — one rotation per measure.
  • Rings: concentric circles; the angle is the position in the bar, like a clock face.
  • Petals: flower curves where position follows elapsed time.
  • Polygon: rotating shape where the position along the edge is time.

Timeline — point-based layouts (advance with each hit)

  • Fibonacci: golden-ratio spiral growing from the center.
  • Radial: 8-fold mirrored patterns, great for mandalas.

Timeline & Timing

  • Press ▶ Start (or P) to run the timeline. Turn on Metronome for a click.
  • The metronome is sample-accurate — it's scheduled on the audio clock, so it doesn't drift even when the screen is busy.
  • Hits are placed using the MIDI timestamp of each note, not the moment the screen redraws.
  • Every kit has a little delay between hitting a pad and the note arriving. Use the Offset setting's Calibrate… button and play along with 16 clicks; Drum Painter measures your average delay and fills in the offset.
  • Turn on Timing Graph to see how far each hit was from the grid, in milliseconds: + means late, − means early.

Blend Modes

Control how new paint combines with what's already on the canvas:

  • Normal: standard opaque painting
  • Multiply: darkens, like overlapping ink
  • Screen: lightens, additive brightness
  • Overlay: boosts contrast, vivid results
  • Dodge: brightens highlights, glowing effect
  • Burn: deepens shadows
  • Add: additive light for bright glowing overlaps
  • Difference: inverts colors for psychedelic effects

Drum Customization

Click Customize Drums for per-drum settings:

  • Color: base color for this drum — or use 🎨 Apply Palette to recolor several drums at once
  • Size: size multiplier (30% to 300%)
  • Opacity: transparency (10% to 100%)
  • Shape: Brushstroke, Splatter, Ink Blot, Circle, Square, Triangle, Hexagon, Star, Diamond
  • Hue Variation: color shift (0° to 60°) — harder hits shift further
  • Rotation: angle of shapes (0–360°)
  • Stroke: optional outline with its own weight (1–10px) and color

Recording & Export

  • Video: press Space or the record button. Choose quality first. Recordings appear under “Recent Recordings”, where you can download, merge with audio, or delete them. The metronome, timing graph and bottom panel are left out of the video.
  • Sync & Merge: click “Sync & Merge” on a recording to load an audio file, preview video and audio together with an adjustable offset, then merge. The merge runs entirely in your browser.
  • Image: press S to save the canvas as a PNG.
  • Timeline recording: check “Record video with timeline” under Recording Options to capture a take automatically when the timeline starts.

Settings & Projects

Auto-save: settings save to your browser about 2 seconds after any change. The dot next to the MIDI status shows the state: yellow = pending, green = saved.

Export / Import: use the buttons at the bottom of Customize Drums to save your setup to a JSON file or load one — handy for backups and sharing setups with other drummers.

Reset to Factory: restores all settings to defaults (asks first).

Projects: save complete named setups (drum settings, mappings and visual preferences) and load them from the dropdown.

⚡ Power User Guide

Device-Specific MIDI Mapping

Each drum can be mapped to a MIDI note + device combination, which prevents conflicts when several controllers send the same note numbers.

  • Exact match: the drum only triggers from that device + note
  • Any device: clear the device mapping to accept the note from any source
  • Bank mode: pad and fader assignments are device-specific too

Velocity Scaling Curves

Apply curves to how velocity affects size, opacity and hue variation:

  • Fixed: ignores velocity; always uses the slider value
  • 1:1: linear response
  • Boost Soft: amplifies soft hits (logarithmic) — more sensitivity at low velocities
  • Gate Soft: suppresses soft hits (exponential) — emphasizes harder hits
  • Gate Loud: smooth quadratic curve

Each drum has independent curves for size, opacity and hue variation.

MIDI CC Live Control

Map faders and knobs to parameters in real time:

  • Per-drum (direct mode): each drum can have its own CC controls for size, opacity, hue, rotation and stroke
  • Global (bank mode): faders control the currently selected drum
  • Auto-calibration: move the controller through its full range during the 3-second learn window
  • Pitch bend: mapped as a pseudo-CC (1001–1016 for channels 1–16)

Time-Based Layouts

Staff, Spiral, Rings, Petals and Polygon follow the timeline clock and share these settings:

  • BPM: 30–240, with optional Auto Tempo to speed up or slow down every few bars
  • Time signatures: 2/4 through 12/4
  • Grid: quarter notes, eighth notes, triplets, sixteenths
  • Offset: −50 to 200 ms latency compensation; use Calibrate… to measure it
  • Warm-up: 0–4 measures of count-in before painting begins
  • MIDI Clock out: check “Send MIDI Clock (tempo sync)” to send 24 ppq clock to your DAW via a virtual port such as loopMIDI. The clock follows Auto Tempo changes.

Staff layout

  • Show Grid: hide/show staff lines and pick their color
  • Row Space: vertical spacing between drum lanes (0–50px)
  • Per Row: auto or a fixed number of measures (1–8)

Spiral layout

  • One full rotation per measure, quantized to the beat positions of the time signature
  • Tightness (5–40): space between rotations
  • Separate tracks: give each drum its own lane, with adjustable spacing

Point-Based Layouts

Fibonacci and Radial don't need the timeline running — each hit advances the pattern, which makes them good for live performance.

Data Persistence & Auto-Save

  • Auto-save: settings save about 2 seconds after any change
  • Save indicator: the dot next to the MIDI status (yellow = pending, blue = saving, green = saved, grey = idle)
  • Force save: press D
  • Export/Import: buttons at the bottom of Customize Drums
  • Named projects: keep several complete setups
  • What's saved: drum settings and order, bank mode state, custom drums, timeline/spiral settings and global preferences

Browser & Device Support

  • MIDI works in Chrome, Edge and Opera, and in Firefox 108 or later (Firefox asks you to approve a site-permission add-on the first time).
  • No MIDI in Safari on the Mac or in any browser on iPhone/iPad — Apple doesn't support Web MIDI. You can still try the app there with Keyboard pads or Demo beat.
  • Phones and tablets work for exploring and watching; for playing a kit, use a desktop browser.
